# Sprint Velocity Calculation – E-Learning Platform

## 1. Introduction

Velocity measures the number of story points completed by a Scrum team during a sprint.

Only stories that satisfy the Definition of Done are counted as completed.

## 2. Sample Completed-Points Table

The assignment's original completed-points table was not provided. The following is illustrative sample data.

| Story ID | User Story | Story Points | Completion Status | Points Counted |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| US01 | Learner registration | 5 | 100% Done | 5 |
| US02 | Secure login | 8 | 100% Done | 8 |
| US03 | Browse courses | 3 | 100% Done | 3 |
| US04 | Course enrollment | 5 | 80% Complete | 0 |
| **Total** | | **21** | | **16** |

## 3. Velocity Calculation

Velocity = Total Story Points of Completed Stories

Velocity = 5 + 8 + 3

**Sprint Velocity = 16 Story Points**

## 4. Treatment of the 80% Completed Story

US04 has 5 story points and is only 80% complete.

Although most of the development work may be finished, the story has not met the Definition of Done.

Therefore:

- It cannot be counted as completed.
- It contributes 0 points to this sprint's velocity.
- It remains unfinished and must be completed in a future sprint if it is still required.

Story points are not divided according to percentage completion.

## 5. Why Partial Work Is Not Counted

Counting partially completed stories would make velocity misleading.

For example, counting 80% of a 5-point story as 4 points would incorrectly suggest that the team delivered a completed increment.

Scrum velocity counts only completed work that meets the Definition of Done.

## 6. Conclusion

The team's velocity for this illustrative sprint is **16 story points**.

The 5-point story that is 80% complete is excluded from the calculation because it has not met the Definition of Done.
